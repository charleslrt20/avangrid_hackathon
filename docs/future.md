# Winter-peaking future: heat-pump adoption scenarios (task 2.2)

Generated by `scripts/08b_future.py`. Modeling population 33,927 homes, weather year 9/2025-8/2026 (min -5 °F). Today the system peaks in **summer** (79.1 MW) and the winter peak is 72% of it (57.3 MW).

## Key findings

1. **Crossover:** the system becomes winter-peaking once about **20%** of homes add a heat pump (headline donor group B; 24% with the label-free group C, 36% if heat pumps only partly displace fossil heat as in the average labeled home).
2. **At 30% adoption** the winter peak reaches 89.9 MW vs a 79.1 MW summer peak (today's winter: 57.3 MW). New heat-pump load is 39% of the winter coincident peak; morning hours rise from 6% to 36% of the top winter hours, and the peak sits in a cold snap (2026-02-07, -2 °F).
3. **Per adopter:** +2.9 kW at the winter system peak, +6.3 MWh/yr, ≈ 0 in summer (measured from 40 labeled HP homes).
4. **Batteries need coordinated recharge in winter.** With the task-1.6 rule a battery is nearly empty after the first cold-snap day (1.6 kW on day 1, 0.16 kW on day 3+). Letting every battery recharge freely creates a new 108 MW night peak. Coordinated fleet recharge cuts the annual peak from 89.9 to 84.0 MW (strict rule: 87.9 MW): 0.85 kW per home at the winter peak, offsetting 29% of the added heat-pump peak, worth about $240/home/yr at mid placeholder values.
5. **Deep winter cuts need long-duration energy:** cutting the 30%-scenario winter peak by 10 MW takes 65 MWh over 16 h on the worst day, vs 51 MWh over 8 h for the same cut in summer today. Shallow cuts (≤ 5 MW) cost about the same in both seasons.
6. **3 kVA lens:** 20% of adopters would exceed their old peak + 3 kVA, but only for a median 12 h/yr. One battery unit keeps 66% of them within the cap, two units 73%. The rest need a service evaluation or heat-pump load control (strip-heat lockout).
7. **Thermostat DR alone** gives 0.28 kW per adopter at the winter peak after rebound, roughly a third of a coordinated battery, at almost no cost: the first program to launch.

## Method

- **Added load per heat-pump home is measured, not assumed.** For each hour of day h: gap_h = mean load of HP homes − mean load of non-electric-heat homes (n = 25,188), regressed on HDD(T_heat), max(20 °F − T, 0) and CDD. The intercept (size / occupancy difference) is dropped; what remains is the heating (and extra cooling) the HP adds at that temperature and hour. Low-use, very-high-load and PV homes are left out of both groups.
- **Three donor groups** bracket the answer. Headline = **B** (40 homes).

Reading: a full-displacement heat pump adds about 3.2 kW at 0 °F and 6.3 MWh/yr, and nothing in summer (HP homes cool no more than the average non-electric home, so the cooling term is ≈ 0). Labeled HP homes show no steepening below 20 °F, consistent with fossil backup taking over in the cold (dual fuel). Group C was *selected* on a cold-weather slope break, so its steep low-temperature response is partly by construction; it is a check, not the headline.

- **Adopters:** random draws (seed 20261112) from in-model homes with no electric heat today; scenario sets are nested (5% ⊂ 15% ⊂ 30% of 33,927 homes). Each adopter's profile is scaled by a size ratio drawn from the donor homes' own design heating kW (mean 1), so homes differ as real HP homes do while system totals stay N × profile.
- System peaks recomputed per scenario: top 88 hours per season of base load + added HP load.

## Batteries and demand flex against the winter peak (adopter homes)

Realistic dispatch as in task 1.6: 15 called events per season on the coldest forecast days, flat discharge planned from the energy left, SOC carried across days. Winter windows follow each scenario's top hours (a morning window is added once mornings hold ≥ 15% of them). Policies: **strict** = task-1.6 rule (recharge 00-05h up to the home's own p95 load); **relaxed** = recharge 00-05h up to the home's own annual peak, all batteries at once; **coordinated** = recharge 00-05h and 10-13h up to the home's own peak, with total fleet charging throttled under a per-season system limit, found by bisection so that the event-hour peak equals the recharge ceiling (recharge never sets the peak); each battery also spreads its recharge evenly over the off-peak hours. The same homes are also run *before* the heat pump. Capacity value follows the season of the annual peak in each scenario.

## 3 kVA electrification-enablement lens

Rule from the brief: on 200 A service, ≤ 3 kVA of added load needs no further evaluation (PF 1.0 assumed, so kVA ≈ kW on hourly data). Cap = the home's own pre-adoption hourly peak + 3 kVA. A battery discharges whenever the home would exceed the cap and recharges 00-05h only up to the cap, with SOC carried hour by hour across cold snaps.
