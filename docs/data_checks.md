## (d) Labeled EVs: L2, L1, mid-year adoption, or no signature

Detectors (each compares a customer with their own baseline; detectors are checked against unlabeled customers, whose base rate shows how much each one lifts):

- **L2:** runs of 1-6 consecutive hours at least 3 kWh above the customer's own 20th-percentile load for that month and hour, peaking at least 5.5 kWh above it, counting only hours at 40-80 F. *L2 detectable* = at least 0.5 blocks per week of such hours.
- **L1:** mild nights (00-06h mean 45-70 F) where at least 5 of 6 hours sit 1.0-2.2 kWh above the customer's quiet-night level (20th percentile), with a flat profile. *L1 detectable* = not L2 and at least 25% of mild nights.
- **Mid-year step:** median daily kWh on mild days (50-68 F) in Apr-Jun 2026 vs Sep-Nov 2025 (both shoulder seasons, so seasonality cannot fake it); step-up = at least +5 kWh/day and +30%. **L2 onset** = under 0.1 L2 blocks/week in Sep-Nov and at least 0.5/week in Apr-Jun.

## Implications for feature design (task 1.2)

- PV features use exact-zero structure (midday zero share tracking the sun, night zeros near 0), never negatives. PV-home load is net import; their peak and battery numbers are lower bounds.
- EV baselines must use a low quantile (20th percentile) of the customer's own month x hour load, not the median, or frequent chargers hide inside their own baseline.
- EV output is three-way (L2 / L1 / no home-charging signature). The large no-signature share of labeled EVs is reported as a finding, and recall is reported per class.
- Mid-year adoption is a minor explanation (few L2 onsets) and the shoulder-season step test does not separate labeled EVs from everyone else, so it is not used as an EV feature; keep it only as an 'emerging load' segment signal.
- Flat-zero meters are mostly vacant or disconnected, not broken: report them as a vacant/inactive segment.
