- No geography, feeder, transformer, premise type, tariff, or account dates exist. The only keys are customer ID (to labels) and timestamp (to weather). All customers share one weather series, so weather explains *when* load moves, and differences *between* customers come from their sensitivity to it.
- Weather time alignment: hour_ending h covers clock [h-1, h); weather is joined as the mean of the h-1 and h readings. DST: AMI omits 2025-11-02 HE2 (fall-back duplicate dropped) and 2026-03-08 HE3 (spring-forward); weather duplicates 2025-11-02 01:00 (averaged).
- Missing keys: 1,487 of the 35,725 IDs in the brief have no AMI rows (checked against raw zip rows). Label rows for those IDs cannot be used.

## 4. Data quality

- Coverage: 97.8% of customers have >=364 days; 373 start late (move-in / new meter), 178 end early (move-out); 91 have <180 days. Matrix NaN share 0.63%.
- Annualised use: median 6,699 kWh, p5 1,827, p95 18,648, max 193,008.
- Outliers: 31 customers peak above 25 kWh/h (max 70.1; a 200 A / 240 V service tops out near 48 kW, typical homes peak at 5-12); 30 use >50 MWh/yr. Likely non-residential or multi-unit.
- Near-vacant: 597 customers <1,000 kWh/yr; 184 have >50% zero hours.
- Stuck / flat meters: 233 customers repeat the previous hour's exact value in >50% of hours.
- Zeros: 2,791,131 zero-kWh hours overall; 13% of them belong to the 397 labeled PV customers (delivered-only metering).
- Weather: 98 imputed interval-hours (2026-03-08..03-11 gap), flagged.
- Class balance (implied by the 25% sampling): EV ~744 (2.2%), HP ~352 (1.0%), PV 397 (1.2%). EV/HP unlabeled customers are a mix of negatives and ~75% of positives (PU setting).
- Label rate (%) by ID range / zip chunk (a skew would hint at sampling leakage):

| chunk | label_ev | label_hp | label_pv |
|---|---|---|---|
| (0, 10000] | 0.39 | 0.19 | 0.8 |
| (10000, 20000] | 0.54 | 0.33 | 0.94 |
| (20000, 30000] | 0.52 | 0.26 | 1.33 |
| (30000, 35725] | 0.86 | 0.26 | 1.81 |

- Leakage risks: (1) if the utility's 'likely EV/HP' lists were themselves produced from AMI heuristics, a model can score well by re-learning their rule; (2) any validation that lets features see the whole year cannot claim forecasting skill, so forecasting tests need a time split; (3) customer ID carries no meaning and is excluded.
- PII: no names, addresses, or locations. Hourly load still reveals occupancy and routines, and the brief restricts use to this event, so raw rows must not leave the laptop (no raw data in git, no raw rows sent to hosted LLM APIs; aggregated outputs only).
