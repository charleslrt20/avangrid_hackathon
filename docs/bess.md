# Behind-the-meter battery and demand-flexibility screening (task 1.6)

Generated by `scripts/08_bess.py`. Battery: 2 x Enphase IQ 10C = 20.16 kWh, 7.68 kW continuous, 89.4% round-trip efficiency. **Headline metric: avoided buildout cost** from coincident-peak relief; $ values are PLACEHOLDER ranges in `config.py` to confirm with Avangrid.

## Method

- **Candidates:** modeling population, not low-use/seasonal, with ≥ 1.5 kW of load in the summer or winter system peak hours → **22,682** homes.
- **Dispatch (hour by hour, state of charge carried across days):** recharge 00-05h only, never above the home's own p95 load (no new customer peak); discharge flat at min(7.68 kW, usable energy / hours left), never more than the home's load (no export). Multi-day heat waves and cold snaps run in sequence, so an incomplete overnight recharge reduces the next day's relief.
- **Perfect foresight** (upper bound): discharge exactly in the system's top 88 hours per season.
- **Realistic** (headline): 15 events per season on the hottest / coldest forecast days (forecast error s.d. 2.0 °F), fixed window HE15-20 summer and HE16-21 winter. Events caught: 13/18 summer and 13/20 winter peak days.
- **Relief that counts** = average kW the battery actually delivered across the system's top hours (missed hours count as zero). **Firm kW** = P10 across called event days of the flat level held through the whole window: the sustained kW we claim, not the 7.68 kW nameplate (6 h x 7.68 kW = 46 kWh > 20.16 kWh, so a full window is energy-limited to 3.36 kW).
- **Sizing:** energy and power needed to hold the home at its own seasonal median through the window on each true peak day, P90 across days → no battery / 1 unit / 2 units / 2 units + DR.
- **Demand flex (no battery):** AC shed 30% and electric-heat shed 20% of weather-driven load (rebounds in the 2 hours after the window); EV charging in the window deferred to the next night's 00-05h (managed charging). 100% of shifted energy comes back. Relief is net of rebound.
- **Avoided buildout $/yr** = capacity × relief at the annual system peak (summer today) + transmission × mean(summer, winter relief) + distribution deferral × max(summer, winter relief). $/kW-yr low/mid/high: capacity 30/60/100; transmission 50/100/150; distribution_deferral 25/75/150. NPV over 15 years at 7% (factor 9.11).
- **Priority score** = 0.45 avoided-$ rank + 0.15 peak-day persistence + 0.15 energy-feasible event coverage + 0.15 electrified (EV or electric heat) + 0.10 PV. Tier 1 = top 10% of candidates, Tier 2 next 20%, Tier 3 next 30%.

**Economics, honestly:** a Tier-1 home avoids about $335/yr of buildout at mid values (NPV ≈ $3,050 over 15 years). That covers only part of a two-unit installation, so system-wide avoided cost alone does not justify a battery program. The case rests on (1) placing batteries where a specific feeder or transformer upgrade is deferred (distribution value is location-specific and can be far above the system average), (2) enabling electrification within the 3 kVA headroom (task 2.2), and (3) stacking customer value (backup power, bill savings).
